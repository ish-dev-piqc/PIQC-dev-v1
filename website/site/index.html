The interpretation burden

One protocol lands. Five roles start translating it by hand.

The coordinator turns the Schedule of Assessments into visit worksheets. The nurse pulls out vitals timing and observation rules. The investigator re-reads eligibility criteria. The lab traces sample-handling instructions. The pharmacy reconstructs dosing and accountability requirements. Same document, five parallel manual translations — carried in working memory and local notes, redone at every site.

Protocols will remain complex. The human burden does not have to. Site Mode does the translation once, keeps every line traceable to its source, and hands each role a view it can review — not re-derive.

Visit Execution Workspace

One visit. Seven phases. Every requirement traceable.

PIQC drafts every study visit as a phased, visual checklist that follows the day as it actually unfolds — from Pre-Visit Preparation through Subject Arrival / Check-In, Core Visit Procedures, Dosing / IMP Administration, Post-Dose Observation, and Safety / AE / Conmed Review, to Visit Closeout & Next-Visit Planning. On visits without dosing, the dosing phases adapt to pre- and post-procedure requirements.

  • Every requirement classified — required, conditional, endpoint-critical, safety-critical
  • A confidence signal on every line
  • A § source link to the exact section, page, and quote
  • A completeness pass that flags possibly-missing requirements — you add or dismiss

Classifications use the protocol’s own logic — Required, Conditional, If Applicable, Primary Endpoint, Secondary Endpoint, Safety Critical — and every confidence signal (high, medium, low, needs review) says why, so a coordinator knows exactly which lines deserve a second look before the visit starts.

Completeness signals

A second pass that flags what might be missing.

After the checklist is drafted, PIQC runs a completeness pass against the protocol and flags possibly-missing requirements — items that look protocol-mandated for this visit but were not detected in the parsed checklist. Each signal shows its reasoning and its protocol source.

Then the decision is human: the coordinator reviews each signal and chooses Add as requirement or Dismiss. Nothing is ever added to the checklist automatically. PIQC advises and drafts; humans review, decide, and approve.

Realtime sync

Enroll a participant. The schedule materializes.

Calendar, Visits, and Participants stay in step in realtime. The moment a participant is enrolled, their full visit schedule materializes from the parsed Schedule of Assessments — every visit, every window, already on the calendar the whole team shares.

No re-keying visit dates from the protocol, no reconciling one coordinator’s calendar against another’s. The schedule comes from the same parsed source as the checklists — so what the team sees is what the protocol says.
