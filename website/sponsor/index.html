Parse once, generate many

One reviewed extraction. Every deliverable downstream.

PIQC parses the protocol once into structured, source-cited intelligence, and a human reviews that extraction in the Source-of-Truth Reviewer before anything is generated from it. Every deliverable the engine drafts is an expression of the same reviewed backbone — no one re-derives the protocol’s meaning per document, and every fact stays linked to the page it came from.

  1. Protocol PDF
  2. Structured extraction
  3. Human review (SOTR)
  4. Deliverable Engine
  5. Draft deliverables

AI extracts; humans own interpretation. Accept for draft, edit, reject, or flag — each decision logged with reviewer, timestamp, and version before it feeds a single deliverable.

The Protocol Deliverable Engine

Two deliverables, live today. A shared engine underneath.

Monitoring Prep Checklist

A structured checklist that turns protocol requirements into monitoring preparation focus — endpoint-critical procedures, visit windows, eligibility and prohibited-medication review areas. Every line carries its content-origin label and a link to its protocol source.

Risk Overview

Plain-language sections describing where the protocol is operationally demanding — no opaque scores, just readable framing a study team can review, edit, and release with the facts cited beneath it.

The deliverable set is extensible: the engine, the review flow, and the provenance model are shared, so new deliverable types inherit the same discipline.

Content-origin honesty

Every block says where it came from.

A deliverable that blends protocol text with AI framing — without saying which is which — borrows authority it hasn’t earned. In Sponsor Mode, every block carries one of three content-origin labels, so reviewers always know what they’re reading.

Protocol fact

Grounded in the protocol itself, with the quote, section, and page behind it — one click away.

PIQC framing

Operational framing PIQC derived from protocol facts. Labeled so it is never mistaken for the protocol’s own words.

Human note

Editorial your team adds during review — attributed as human-authored, and preserved through regeneration.

Source provenance

Click a fact. See the page it came from.

Every protocol-fact block opens a source drawer: the quoted passage, its section and page, and the extraction’s confidence — with a “View cited page” control that takes the reviewer to the protocol itself. Trust doesn’t have to be assumed; it can be checked, fact by fact.

Regeneration

Regenerate the draft. Keep the human work.

Protocols change, and drafts get re-run. The engine treats your team’s review as the durable layer.

What regeneration preserves

  • ✓Accepted edits and human notes carry forward.
  • ✓Items your team rejected stay rejected — regeneration cannot resurrect them.
  • ✓Every change lands in the edit log, attributed to a reviewer and a time.

What the engine won’t pretend

  • —Coverage-gap honesty: when a section can’t be grounded in the protocol, the deliverable says so. A visible gap beats a confident blank.
  • —Exports are DRAFT-watermarked PDFs with a requires-human-review disclaimer and a traceability appendix — a draft never dresses up as a released document.
  • —Human sign-off marks an item ready — release decisions stay in your organization’s own process.
